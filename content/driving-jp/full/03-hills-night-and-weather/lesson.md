**Hills and mountain roads.**

- Stopping behind a car on an uphill: don't get too close - it may roll back.
  Starting uphill: use the handbrake.
- Near the **top of a slope** you cannot see: **crawl**, and **no overtaking**.
  No overtaking on a **steep downhill** either.
- Downhill: **low gear and engine braking** (2 or L in an automatic). Riding
  the foot brake down a long slope can make the brakes fail. Leave a longer
  gap - the stopping distance is longer.
- **Meeting on a slope: the car going down gives way** to the car going up,
  because starting uphill is harder. But if there is a passing place near the
  car going up, that car waits in it.
- With a drop on one side and no room to pass safely, the car **on the cliff
  side stops** and gives way.

**Bends.** Slow down on the straight before the bend. Steer gently. Stay on
your side of the centre. Mind the inner wheel difference (内輪差): the rear
wheels cut in. No overtaking on a bend.

**Night.** Vision is poorer and your sense of speed is dulled: go slower than
by day. A pedestrian near the centre of the road can vanish between your lights
and an oncoming car's (蒸発現象). Look far ahead. Lights on early at dusk.

**Lights.**

- On at night - and by day in a tunnel or thick fog where you cannot see
  **50 m** ahead (**200 m** on an expressway).
- **Main beam** normally, except in busy built-up streets. **Dip** (or dim)
  when meeting an oncoming car or driving right behind another.
- Dazzled by oncoming lights: shift your eyes a little to the **left front**.
- Before a blind junction or bend, main beam or a flash announces you.
- Interior light off while moving (buses excepted).
- Parked or stopped on a road at night: hazard lights, parking lights or tail
  lights on - unless street lighting makes the car visible from 50 m behind,
  or a warning triangle or light is set out.

**Rain.** Slower, longer gaps, nothing sudden. Paving is slipperiest when rain
has **just started**; steel plates at roadworks and tram rails too. Slow down
so as not to splash pedestrians - that one is an obligation. Avoid deep
puddles: water in the brake drums weakens the brakes.

**Snow and ice.** Chains or winter tyres. Where the chains sign is up, no
chains means no entry. Follow the tracks of earlier cars.

**Fog.** Fog lamps - or headlights if the car has none - on early; follow the
centre line, guard rail or the tail lights ahead; slow down; use the horn if
needed.

**When things go wrong.**

- **Puncture**: grip the wheel, keep the car straight, no hard braking - brake
  in short repeated presses.
- **Rear-wheel skid**: don't brake. Ease off the accelerator and steer the way
  the rear is sliding - rear slides right, steer right.
- **Brakes fail downhill**: change down quickly and pull the handbrake; failing
  that, a wheel into the hillside ditch, the body along the guard rail, or
  into gravel.
- **Head-on danger**: horn and brake together, as far left as possible; leave
  the road if what is beside it is not dangerous.
- **Engine racing and stuck**: neutral, to the roadside, stop, then switch off.
