Same format as the town scenes: a scene, three statements, **all three right
to score**.

Outside town the hazards change from people stepping out to **speed,
distance and grip**:

- **Night**: you see less and later, and your sense of speed is dulled. A
  pedestrian between two sets of headlights can vanish.
- **Rain**: the stopping distance grows; at expressway speed the tyres can
  float (hydroplaning).
- **Railway crossings**: stop, look and listen; don't start across unless
  there is room for your car on the far side.
- **Expressways**: get up to speed in the acceleration lane, don't hinder the
  main carriageway, and keep about your speed in metres as a gap - double in
  the wet.
- **Mountain roads**: engine braking downhill; slow before the bend, not in
  it; expect the oncoming car over the centre line.

The pattern of the statements is the same. Easing off and leaving room is
right; hurrying, and trusting the other driver to sort it out, is wrong.

Source: 交通の方法に関する教則, 第6章 and 第7章.
