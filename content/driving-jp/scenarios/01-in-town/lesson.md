The last five questions of the full licence test (本免) are **illustration
questions** (イラスト問題): a picture of a scene from the driver's seat, and
three statements about what to do or what to watch for. Each is worth
**2 points**, and you score **only if all three answers are right**.

The real test draws the scene. Here it is described in words; read it and
picture it before answering.

**How to read a scene.** Ask three things, in this order:

1. **What can I not see?** Behind the stopped bus, in front of the parked car,
   behind the oncoming truck. Something may come out of every hidden place.
2. **What might the others do?** The child runs, the cyclist wobbles, the door
   opens, the bus pulls out.
3. **What does the rule say?** Stop before passing a car stopped at a
   crosswalk. Stop or crawl for a child alone or an elderly person with a
   stick. Give way to straight-on and left-turning traffic when turning right.

**How the statements are written.** Almost always:

- a statement that names a hidden danger and slows down for it - **true**;
- a statement that trusts someone else to stay out of the way, or speeds up
  to get clear - **false**;
- a statement that uses the **horn** to make people move - **false**. The horn
  is for danger, not for clearing your path.

"Slow down", "be ready to stop", "wait" are rarely wrong. "Accelerate",
"quickly", "they will surely stop" rarely right. But read each statement to
the end: a true opening can be followed by a wrong action.
