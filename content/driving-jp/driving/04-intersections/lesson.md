title: Intersections
summary: Turning left and right, who gives way at uncontrolled junctions, roundabouts, and not getting stuck in the box.

Intersections are where most accidents happen. Watch for turning cars and
pedestrians; turning right, watch especially for motorcycles coming straight
on, which are easy to miss. Turning cars cut the corner with their rear wheels
(内輪差) - large vehicles most of all - so watch the left for pedestrians and
cyclists.

**Turning left**: keep as far left as you can beforehand, and crawl along the
edge of the intersection.

**Turning right**: move as close to the centre as you can beforehand, and crawl
just inside the centre of the intersection. From a one-way road, keep to the
right edge. **Cars going straight or turning left go first**, even if you
entered the intersection before them. Follow arrows painted on the road and
lane-by-direction signs.

**Don't enter** an intersection - even on green - if the traffic ahead could
leave you stopped in it, blocking crossing traffic. The same goes for "keep
clear" markings (停止禁止部分, e.g. in front of police and fire stations),
crosswalks and railway crossings.

**No signals** (交通整理の行われていない交差点):

- The crossing road is the **priority road** or **wider**: crawl, and let its
  traffic go first.
- Roads of about the same width: give way to **trams** and to **vehicles coming
  from your left** (左方車).
- **Stop sign** (一時停止): stop just before the stop line (or the
  intersection), and let crossing traffic go first. A flashing red means the
  same. A flashing yellow: go on with care.

**Roundabouts** (環状交差点). Whatever your direction, keep to the left edge
and crawl along the edge. Entering, crawl and **give way to traffic already
inside**. Signal left to leave (from passing the exit before yours).

Source: 交通の方法に関する教則, 第5章第7節 and 第5節1(2).
