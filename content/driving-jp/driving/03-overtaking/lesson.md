title: Overtaking

**Overtaking** (追越し) means changing course to get in front of a moving
vehicle ahead. (Passing a vehicle without changing course is 追い抜き.)

**Never overtake when**:

- the car ahead is itself overtaking a motor vehicle (二重追越し);
- the car ahead is moving right to turn right;
- going onto the right side would get in the way of oncoming traffic, or you
  could only get back in by blocking the car you passed;
- the car behind has started to overtake you.

**Never overtake a car or moped** (changing course or drawing level) at:

- a "no overtaking" sign;
- near a bend; near the top of an uphill slope, or on a steep downhill slope;
- in a tunnel - unless it has lanes;
- an intersection and the 30 m before it - unless you are on the priority road;
- a railway crossing, crosswalk or bicycle crossing, and the 30 m before it.

A sign or marking can also forbid crossing to the right side to overtake.

**How**: pass on the **right**. Pass on the **left** a car that has moved to the
centre (or the right edge of a one-way road) to turn right, and trams. Keep a
safe gap. The steps: check it's allowed; check ahead and right-behind (and
oncoming traffic if crossing over); right indicator; after about 3 seconds
accelerate gently out, within the limit; pass; left indicator; carry on until
you can see the car you passed in the **rear-view mirror**; move back gently;
cancel the signal.

**Being overtaken**: don't speed up until the other car has finished, and if
there isn't room, keep left and let it by.

**Cutting in** (割込み): don't cut in front of, or cross in front of, cars
stopped or crawling at an intersection or railway crossing. Don't squeeze a car
travelling alongside (幅寄せ).

Source: 交通の方法に関する教則, 第5章第6節.
