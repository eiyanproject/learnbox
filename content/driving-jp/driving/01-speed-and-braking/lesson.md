title: Speed, stopping and braking
summary: The legal maximum speeds, what stopping distance is made of, how to brake, and where you must crawl.

**Maximum speed** (最高速度). Obey the limit shown by signs or markings. Where
none is shown, a car may not go over **60 km/h** on ordinary roads; a moped
(一般原動機付自転車) not over **30 km/h**. Expressways have their own limits (see
the expressway topic). Within the limit, still drive at a speed safe for the
road, traffic, weather and visibility.

**Stopping distance** (停止距離) = **reaction distance** (空走距離: from sensing
danger until the brakes start to work) + **braking distance** (制動距離: from
then until the car stops).

- Tired: reaction takes longer, so the reaction distance grows.
- Wet road, or a heavy load: the braking distance grows.
- Wet road **and** worn tyres: stopping distance can be about **twice** that on
  a dry road with good tyres.

Keep a following distance (車間距離) that lets you stop if the car ahead stops
suddenly.

**Braking**. Press gently at first, then harder as needed. Brake in several
presses - especially on slippery roads; it also flashes your brake lights at
the car behind. No sudden braking except to avoid danger. With **ABS**, in an
emergency press hard at once and **keep pressing**. Slow down with the
accelerator first where you can.

**Crawl** (徐行: a speed at which you can stop at once) at:

- a "slow" sign (徐行の標識)
- an intersection where you can't see left and right - unless it has signals
  or you are on the priority road (優先道路)
- near a bend
- near the top of an uphill slope, and on a steep downhill slope

Source: 交通の方法に関する教則, 第5章第4節.
