Crossings (踏切) produce the worst accidents, so the rule is strict.

**Stop and check - every time.** Stop just before the crossing (just before the
stop line if there is one) and check left and right with **your own eyes and
ears**, opening the window if it helps. A train that has just passed can hide
another coming the other way. **Following the car ahead does not excuse you**:
you stop and check too.

The one exception: a crossing with **traffic lights**. There you follow the
signal and may go through on green without stopping.

**Do not enter** when the alarm is sounding, or the barrier is down or has
started to come down. Do not enter if the far side is so congested that you
could be left stopped on the tracks.

**Crossing**: to avoid stalling, **don't change gear** - go straight across in
the low gear you set off in. Keep **slightly towards the centre** so a wheel
does not drop off the edge, watching for pedestrians and oncoming cars.

**Stopped on the tracks** (踏切で故障): warn the train driver at once, and get
the car off the crossing.

1. Where there is an alarm, use the push-button emergency device (踏切支障報知装置)
   on the alarm post.
2. Where there is none, signal with the flare (発炎筒) you carry.
3. With no flare, burn something smoky nearby as a signal.

In a manual car, a last resort is to put it in first or second gear and move it
on the starter motor - this does not work in an automatic, or in a car that will
not start without the clutch pressed.

Source: 交通の方法に関する教則, 第6章第1節 and 第5節(1).
